Capital market assumptions

Correlations

By default correlations come from your data. Tick the box to type your own; edit the cells below the diagonal and the matrix stays symmetric. If the numbers are not mutually consistent, the nearest valid matrix is used and you are told.

Mandate

The mandate sets what the portfolio may hold: how much is invested, whether it can short or borrow, and what its risk is measured against. Every later step follows it.

Constraints

Minimum and maximum weight for each asset are set in the table at the top (a negative minimum allows a short position, if the mandate permits). Group limits cap or floor the combined weight of several assets, for example all equities.

Efficient frontier

The curve shows the lowest-risk portfolio for each level of return under your constraints. Markers are reference portfolios. Hover for weights.

Portfolios

These portfolios carry into every later step. Add an optimised portfolio for a specific objective, or type your own weights.

Backtest

Applies each portfolio's weights to the actual returns in your data over the analysis period.

Attribution

Shows how much each asset added to the portfolio's return. With a benchmark it also shows the allocation effect: the return gained or lost by holding more or less of each asset than the benchmark. Both portfolios hold the same assets, so there is no selection effect.

Historical stress windows

How the chosen portfolio behaved in named crises covered by your data (from the Dot-Com bust to the mid-2026 emerging-market sell-off), plus drawdowns detected in the portfolio itself, so new episodes appear automatically as the data updates.

Shock severity

Stressed asset return = base assumption + severity × (return in the window − base assumption). 1× reproduces the window; 2× is twice as severe.

Monte Carlo simulation

Projects the portfolio forward using your assumptions. Normal and fat-tailed draws use the expected returns and covariance; bootstrap resamples blocks of your actual history.

Spending policy

Projects a fund that earns the portfolio's return, pays out under a spending rule and receives contributions. All values are in today's money and start at 100. The key question is how likely the fund is to keep its inflation-adjusted value.

Currency hedge

For a holding priced in a foreign currency. Pick the column that holds the asset in its own currency and the column that holds the exchange rate (the value of the foreign currency in your base currency). The hedge sells the foreign currency forward on a share of the position.
